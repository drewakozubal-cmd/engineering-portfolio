SHEET 01 / 03

Moving Operations Platform

Live 2026 — Present · Founder & Developer

A moving company runs on a dozen disconnected tools — a spreadsheet for quotes, a group text for scheduling, a separate app for payroll. I built a single multi-tenant platform that carries a job from the first phone call to the final paycheck, with each company's data walled off behind role-based access.

  • Multi-tenant PostgreSQL/Prisma data model with company-level isolation and role-based access control.
  • Automated the handoff between sales, scheduling, and payroll so a booked job creates its own crew assignment and pay record.
  • Integrated mapping/travel-time, email, and payment-processing APIs into one workflow.
  • Automated testing covering authentication, cross-company data isolation, and API endpoints before each production deploy.

Stack

  • Next.js / TypeScript
  • PostgreSQL + Prisma
  • Vercel + Neon
  • REST APIs, AI/LLM integration

Result

  • Crew size: 3 → 5
  • Revenue: ~1.5× MoM growth
  • Env: dev / preview / prod on Git CI
SHEET 02 / 03

Wind Turbine Test Stand

Junior Year · Collaborative Engineering, Messiah University

Before you can trust a turbine's numbers, you need a rig that won't lie to you. Our team built on-site and off-site test stands to hold a wind turbine steady under load while we measured how it actually performed — not just how the spec sheet said it would.

  • Ran stress calculations and structural analysis to size test-stand components before anything got cut.
  • Modeled every component and assembly in SolidWorks, then fabricated and assembled the physical stands.
  • Instrumented the rig and ran system tests, troubleshooting both the mechanical mounting and the turbine's electrical output as issues came up.

Scope

  • On-site + off-site stands
  • Structural & stress analysis
  • Mechanical + electrical test

Tools

  • SolidWorks/CAD
  • Hand fabrication
SHEET 03 / 03

Manual Washing Machine

In progress Senior Year · Collaborative Engineering, Messiah University

No electricity, no washing machine — that's the constraint the field asked us to design around. I'm leading a subgroup building a manually powered washing machine for refugee communities in Greece, where the deciding factor isn't performance, it's whether a part can be replaced with what's actually on hand.

  • Taking the machine from concept through engineering analysis and SolidWorks CAD, with fabrication and physical testing to follow.
  • Designing the drum, shaft, bearings, structural frame, seals, and hand-operated mechanism against strength, manufacturability, cost, and durability.
  • Every material and fastener choice is filtered through one question: can it be sourced and repaired locally after we're gone.
Field site: Greece

Subsystems

  • Drum
  • Shaft & bearings
  • Structural frame
  • Seals
  • Hand-operated mechanism

Design drivers

  • No electricity required
  • Local materials only
  • Field maintainable

Case Study
PLTR

Palantir Technologies

Figures as of Q2 2026 earnings (reported Aug 2026)
Thesis

Believed the market underweighted Palantir's durable government/defense contract base and underestimated how quickly its AIP platform would drive commercial revenue acceleration.

Financials

Revenue grew 93% YoY in Q2 2026 (record quarter); ~86% adjusted gross margin; $1.22B adjusted free cash flow (+115% YoY, ~63% FCF margin); $9.2B cash, no debt.

Valuation

Never cheap on traditional multiples (trailing P/E ~148, forward P/E ~92) — the bet was that accelerating growth and margin expansion would justify the multiple rather than the multiple compressing.

Competitive Position

Ontology-based data integration creates high switching costs once embedded in a customer's workflow; sticky long-term government contracts; early mover in operational AI for defense and enterprise.

Risks Identified

Premium valuation vulnerable to multiple compression; revenue concentration in government/defense; heavy stock-based compensation; execution risk on the commercial-growth thesis.

Decision

Bought and held through volatility rather than trimming on valuation concerns, betting that fundamentals would keep validating the thesis.

Outcome

Entry at ~$20/share; fundamentals (revenue growth, FCF generation) have validated the original thesis so far, though the stretched valuation remains a risk I'm actively monitoring rather than a settled question.
