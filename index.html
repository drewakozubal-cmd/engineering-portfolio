In Progress

Manual Washing Machine

Senior Year · Collaborative Engineering, Messiah University · Field site: Greece
The Problem

Design a manually powered washing machine for refugee communities with limited access to electricity. The current project is focused on deployment in Greece.

My Role

Mechanical design, SolidWorks CAD, component selection, engineering analysis, manufacturability, and cost considerations.

Current Design

The design uses a horizontal perforated inner drum, an outer enclosure, a hand-driven shaft and crank, bearing supports, and a steel support frame. It's being developed around durability, field serviceability, corrosion resistance, safety, low cost, and locally sourceable components.

Engineering Work

Work so far includes shaft and bearing design, loading and stress calculations, drum design, frame design, seals, material selection, and vibration considerations, along with a manufacturability review. Analysis and validation are ongoing, this is still an active senior design project.

Wind Turbine Test Stand

Junior Year · Collaborative Engineering, Messiah University

Our team needed a way to check whether a wind turbine actually performed the way its spec sheet claimed. We built on-site and off-site test stands to hold the turbine steady under load so we could measure its real output.

  • Ran stress calculations and structural analysis to size test-stand components before anything got cut.
  • Modeled every component and assembly in SolidWorks, then fabricated and assembled the physical stands.
  • Instrumented the rig and ran system tests, troubleshooting both the mechanical mounting and the turbine's electrical output as issues came up.

Moving Operations Platform

2026 to Present · Founder & Developer · Live

A moving company runs on a dozen disconnected tools: a spreadsheet for quotes, a group text for scheduling, a separate app for payroll. I built a single multi-tenant platform that carries a job from the first phone call to the final paycheck, with each company's data walled off behind role-based access.

  • Multi-tenant PostgreSQL/Prisma data model with company-level isolation and role-based access control.
  • Automated the handoff between sales, scheduling, and payroll so a booked job creates its own crew assignment and pay record.
  • Integrated mapping/travel-time, email, and payment-processing APIs into one workflow.
  • Automated testing covering authentication, cross-company data isolation, and API endpoints before each production deploy.

Stack: Next.js/TypeScript, PostgreSQL + Prisma, Vercel + Neon, REST APIs, AI/LLM integration. Results: crew size grew from 3 to 5, revenue grew roughly 1.5× month over month, with dev/preview/prod environments on Git CI.

PLTR

Palantir Technologies

Thesis: I thought the market was underestimating Palantir's government business and how much its new AI platform (AIP) could accelerate commercial growth.

Valuation at Entry: I bought around $20 in June 2024. Even then it wasn't a cheap stock, trading at a very high earnings multiple. I wasn't buying it because the valuation looked cheap. I believed the government business was durable and that AIP could grow commercial revenue enough to justify paying a premium.

What Happened Afterward: The business accelerated after I bought in. Q2 2024 results, reported that August, showed 27% year-over-year revenue growth, with U.S. commercial revenue up 55%, U.S. government revenue up 24%, and customer count up 41%. They also had about $4.0B in cash and short-term treasuries. That gave me more evidence the commercial AI thesis was playing out, so I kept holding.

Competitive Position: Once a customer builds their workflow around Palantir's ontology system, it's hard and expensive to switch away from, so I think they have real staying power there. They also had long, sticky government contracts and got into operational AI for defense and enterprise early.

Risks: The stock is priced for a lot to keep going right, so if growth ever slows there's a real risk of the multiple getting cut. Government contracts remain a major part of the business, they use a lot of stock-based compensation, and the commercial growth story still has to keep playing out.

Decision: I held through the swings instead of selling on valuation worries, because I was betting the fundamentals would keep proving the thesis right.

Outcome: I got in around $20 a share. The fundamentals since then, revenue growth and free cash flow, have backed up what I believed going in, but the valuation is stretched enough that I'm still watching it closely rather than treating this as settled.

Current (Q2 2026): Revenue up 93% year over year. Gross margin around 86%. $1.22B in adjusted free cash flow, up 115% year over year, with a 63% FCF margin. $9.2B in cash and short-term U.S. Treasuries.
